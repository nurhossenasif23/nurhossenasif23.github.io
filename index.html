Dual-Receiver GNSS Integrity Monitoring: Identifiability, Calibration Transfer, and Fallback Gating

NUR Asif · Md. Rishad Ahmed · Muhammad Shihab Khan · Arafat Hossain Mahin · Sajjat Shahriar · Abu Hanif Siam

IEEE ICCIT 2026

A simulation study of the blind spot in dual-receiver GNSS differencing, cross-source anomaly monitoring, and whether a fallback navigation source is healthy enough to use.

Research summary & findings

Approach

A common position offset cancels when two GNSS receivers are differenced, so agreement does not establish correctness. This study compares weighted CUSUM, vector snapshot, and vector EWMA monitors with disjoint synthetic fitting, calibration, and evaluation runs. Thresholds target a 1% false-alarm probability over a complete clean 100-second run. It then evaluates calibration transfer under changed temporal dependence and tests a separately calibrated health diagnostic before fallback to a local reference.

Reported findings

For the tested 6 m common-step scenario, weighted CUSUM detected 999 of 1,000 held-out events with a conditional median delay of 0.3 s. Calibration did not transfer reliably to temporally correlated errors: the EWMA clean-run alarm proportion rose to 100% in the tested AR(1) setting.

Scope & limitations

All results concern specified synthetic position-output experiments. They do not validate RF spoofing detection, recorded-data performance, navigation safety, or closed-loop control. A shared displacement of both local references defeated the tested health check.

Summary based on the author-provided manuscript. Simulation results are not field validation.

02Accepted for publication
2026

Sustaining Service in Post-Flood UAV Relay Networks: Battery Handover, Return Reserve, and Spare-Fleet Sizing Under UAV Failures

IEEE ICCIT 2026

A MATLAB simulation examining how return-energy reserves, battery handovers, reusable spares, and UAV failures jointly affect post-flood communication service.

Research summary & findings

Approach

A time-stepped MATLAB framework couples air-to-ground access, multi-hop backhaul to a surviving base station, rotary-wing energy consumption, stochastic permanent UAV failures, battery swapping, and a finite spare pool. An energy-threshold handover policy with a return-energy reserve is evaluated against four baselines; two baselines are tuned on disjoint random seeds. The emphasis is service continuity over time rather than instantaneous coverage.

Reported findings

Across 50 Monte Carlo trials in the reported setting, the proposed policy maintained service for 94.1% of a two-hour mission versus 78.0% and 75.7% for the two tuned baselines. The advantage occurred with scarce spares (2–5) and moderate-to-frequent failures.

Scope & limitations

This is a simulation evaluation, not field network validation or a new handover primitive. Without failures, or with six or more spares, the tuned baselines matched the proposed policy.

Summary based on the author-provided manuscript. Simulation results are not field validation.
